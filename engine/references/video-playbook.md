# The video's method, step by step

Source: "How to Buy Property in Morocco in 2026 (Step-by-Step)", Moroccan Brothers (Jamal), YouTube `C0MdBR3jLdM`, published 14 Nov 2025, 1h34m. Recorded around March 2025 (he says "we're in March right now"). Timestamps are h:mm:ss.

This file records **what he teaches**, faithfully. Where his figure or claim did not survive checking, the line ends with **→ see corrections** and the fix is in `corrections-and-gaps.md`. Use his *process* (it is sound); use the *verified numbers* from `assets/defaults.json` and the other reference files.

Keep in mind who is speaking: he sells a mentorship programme, sources deals for a fee and develops off-plan projects he sells to investors. His incentives run toward "buy now". Treat the enthusiasm as marketing and the process as the useful part.

## The spine (0:10:09)

**Search → View → Offer → Notary → Ownership.** Decide how you are paying (cash / mortgage / Islamic finance) *before* you offer, and analyse the deal *before* you view.

## 1. Search (0:11:13 – 0:15:17)

- **Mubawab** — filter by city, property type, budget.
- **Avito** — more private sellers; "not my favourite, but I have bought many properties" there.
- **Facebook groups** — search `<area> immobilier`, join the groups. More direct-to-owner, mostly finished and titled property. He rates this highly.
- Sites and adverts are in French/Arabic; use the browser's translate.
- Keep a running list of what you like and don't.

## 2. Screen before you travel (0:15:17, 1:03:18 – 1:06:21, 1:14:25)

Three questions to put to the agent *before* viewing:

1. **"Is it titled?"** (1:04:18) — if the answer is *melkia* (unregistered, traditional deeds), a beginner should walk away. Melkia can be 30–40% cheaper, but ownership can be challenged by heirs of whoever held the land, and it takes a very good lawyer to check how the deeds were written.
2. **"Is it off-plan?"** (1:15:26) — if you need a mortgage, ask for something that is not off-plan.
3. **Exactly where is it?** (1:19:27) — you need the precise location to find rental comparables on a map.

## 3. View (0:15:17 – 0:18:17)

One test: **"Is it what it says it is?"**

- *Sold as new*: it must look new. Check the finish — chipped paint, peeling wallpaper, flickering lights, loose panelling. Flick every switch, run every tap, push on fitted furniture.
- *Older building*: does everything work, is there damp, is the inside new but the building old?
- *The street*: graffiti, tired shops, mud or unmade roads.
- Photos do not show everything; someone has to stand in it.
- He does not check what "only a builder would look at". **→ see corrections** (structure and seismic checks matter in Marrakech).

## 4. Work out what to offer (0:18:17 – 0:22:20)

Morocco prices by the square metre.

1. Asking price ÷ surface = asking price per m².
2. Find the going rate per m² for that neighbourhood: WhatsApp **7–8 local agents** and ask what one-, two- and three-bed flats sell for per m². The answers cluster.
3. Going rate × surface = what it is worth. "Just because the owner wants that doesn't mean it's worth that."
4. A premium over the going rate is acceptable only if something concrete explains it. His example: a premium of about £6,300 on a furnished new-build was fine because the furniture was worth more than that.

You can do all of this remotely over WhatsApp, in English or with Google Translate into French.

## 5. Decide how you pay — before offering (0:23:20 – 0:36:31, 0:50:09 – 0:56:13)

| | Person A | Person B | Person C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route | Cash | Conventional mortgage | Islamic finance |
| Deposit he quotes | – | 10% Moroccan citizens, 30% foreign investors | 30%, Moroccan citizens only |
| Rate he quotes | – | fixed ~4.3%, variable ~3.9–4.1% | "5-point-something" |
| Term | – | 7–25 years | 7–25 years |

- Get a **mortgage in principle** before you search. He says it lasts 90 days, is renewable, and that the bank cannot withdraw it. **→ see corrections**
- Apply by contacting several banks through their websites; you need not hold an account first.
- Use any mortgage calculator for the monthly payment (his check: 90,000 at 4.3% over 25 years ≈ 490 a month — correct, before VAT on interest).
- **Take the fixed rate, never the variable** (0:39:34). Morocco lets you fix for the whole term. The test: *at the fixed payment, does the deal work — yes or no?* If no, move on; do not bet on rates falling.
- **Rates are negotiable** (0:49:08): the central bank rate was 2.25% when he recorded; a bank charging more than about two points over that is taking too much.
- Islamic finance: he describes a diminishing-ownership structure and says it is closed to foreigners. **→ see corrections**
- Sellers ask "cash or mortgage?" and may want to see the mortgage in principle before taking you seriously.

## 6. Notary and money (0:25:24 – 0:28:25, 0:56:13 – 1:01:16)

- The notary runs the transaction: checks the property, holds the money, transfers title. He says buyer and seller each have one. **→ see corrections**
- **Vet the notary**: a real office you can walk into, Google presence, reviews. "I'd rather pay a bit more." He has heard of notaries cheating buyers.
- **Use a fee simulator before every offer** (he uses a broker's "frais de notaire" simulator). His example on a 100,000 purchase with a mortgage came to about 8.4% — registration duty, land registry, certificates, mortgage registration, the notary's own fee (only about 1%) and VAT.
- **Fees are never financed.** The loan covers the property only. Cash needed = deposit + all fees. His examples on 100,000: 18,379 with a 10% deposit; 38,379 with 30%.
- "Everything I do is simulations. I can work out everything I have to pay before I even go for the property."

## 7. Timing and holding deposits (1:01:16 – 1:03:18)

- Cash: about a week (he has done 24 hours). Mortgage: one to four weeks. **→ see corrections**
- Need more time? Agree a delay with the seller. He once paid a **10% non-refundable deposit** to hold a property for eight weeks; miss the date and the seller keeps it. **→ see corrections** (conditions that make the deposit refundable)

## 8. Agent fees (1:06:21 – 1:08:22)

- Agents charge **2–3.5%** of the price; he tries for 2 and will not go above 3.
- **Both buyer and seller pay.** Add the buyer's share to your cash requirement (his example: 8.4% fees + 3% agent = 11.4%).
- You sign a slip at the viewing promising to pay if you buy, and the notary deducts the fee — so you cannot dodge it.

## 9. Off-plan (1:08:22 – 1:18:27)

- Developers sell in **tranches**: the earliest buyers get the biggest discount (his own example: 30%, then 20%, 15%, 10%) because they carry the most risk; price rises as the building does.
- Typical payment schedule he quotes: about 5,000 MAD application fee, 20% at the start, 20% six weeks later, 30% during construction, 30% on completion.
- **Cash only**: no title exists until completion, so no ordinary mortgage. Only the final instalment can be mortgaged, when the title is issued. Negotiate to push more into that final instalment — he has had 50% and once 70%.
- **Pay late and you lose the money** — it is in the contract and they enforce it.
- **Developers have disappeared with deposits.** Check that the developer has completed several earlier projects.
- A show flat proves nothing about the finished building.
- Adverts that look too cheap, with computer-generated pictures, are off-plan.
- Buyers who got in early then resell at completion ("property trading"); a finished flat you buy from them includes their profit. He accepts that because a finished, titled flat can be mortgaged.

## 10. Analyse the rental deal (1:18:27 – 1:28:33)

His worked example: a new-build two-bed near Avenue Majorelle, Marrakech, asking €140,000.

1. Pin the exact location on Google Maps.
2. Pull nearby short-let listings in **AirDNA** (paid) or by searching Airbnb directly with dates. Open the individual listings; compare interiors, review counts and nightly prices with your property.
3. Ignore listings with no reviews when reading occupancy; give weight to established ones.
4. Settle on a nightly range (his: €50–75, working figure €65).
5. Revenue = nightly rate × occupied nights. He uses 70% occupancy = 21 nights a month. **→ see corrections**
6. Subtract: mortgage, management (10–20%; he uses 10%), electricity (~€60), internet (~€35), TV subscription (~€21). Cleaning is charged to the guest. Optionally 10% contingency. **→ see corrections** (several costs and all taxes are missing, and his mortgage figure is wrong)
7. His result: roughly €650–700 a month profit. **→ see corrections**
8. Keep a spreadsheet: every property, what comparable lets charge.

"Everything's a comparison… the more you do it, the more you understand what places go for."

## 11. Running it afterwards (1:28:33 – 1:31:38)

- **Channel manager** (he uses Guesty): one calendar and price across Airbnb, Booking.com and others.
- **Dynamic pricing** (he uses PriceLabs): raises and lowers nightly price with local demand — school holidays, conferences, events.

## 12. His investment thesis (0:02:01 – 0:10:09) — context, not method

- His father passed on a west London house for £15,000 in the 1990s because it felt expensive; it is now worth £500,000+. Lesson he draws: do not wait for a crash; "better to be in the market than to time the market."
- "Morocco is Western Europe in the 1970s": political stability, diaspora money returning, ties with Europe, ports, renewables, phosphate. He predicts a 100–150k property will be worth over a million within 30–40 years. **→ see corrections** (the actual price record does not support building this into a deal)
