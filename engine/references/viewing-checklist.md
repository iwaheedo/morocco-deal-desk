# Viewing checklist

The video's test is the right frame: **is it what the advert says it is?** His checks come first; the additions cover what he says he skips ("things only a builder would look at") and what a remote buyer cannot see in photos.

Tailor this to the property before handing it to the user: drop what does not apply, and put the items that could kill the deal first. If the user cannot attend, this is the brief for whoever goes on their behalf — ask them to film a continuous walk-through, street included.

## Before going
- [ ] Title number received and ownership certificate ordered (no title number, no trip).
- [ ] Agent's commission agreed in writing.
- [ ] Advert saved (screenshots): surface, floor, what is included.
- [ ] Tape measure or laser, phone charger (to test sockets), torch.

## From the video
**If sold as new** — it must look new:
- [ ] Paint and plaster: chips, cracks, patches, peeling.
- [ ] Every light switch and socket.
- [ ] Every tap, shower and flush; hot water arrives.
- [ ] Fitted furniture, panelling, doors and windows: push, pull, open, close.
- [ ] Lift the rugs; look behind furniture.

**If older**:
- [ ] Does everything work?
- [ ] Damp: stains, smell, bubbling paint — ceilings, bathroom walls, under windows, behind wardrobes.
- [ ] New inside but tired building outside?

**The street**:
- [ ] Graffiti, closed or tired shops, unmade roads, mud, rubbish.
- [ ] What is next door and opposite.

## Additions

**Structure and age**
- [ ] Year of the building permit (ask; check against the title). Before 2002 there was no mandatory seismic code; it was tightened around 2014.
- [ ] Cracks: diagonal cracks at door and window corners, cracks that continue through structural walls or columns, doors that no longer close square.
- [ ] Added floors or rooftop rooms that are not on the plans — a red flag for both structure and legality.
- [ ] Top floor: roof terrace waterproofing, signs of leaks.
- [ ] Old building, medina property, or anything with visible structural cracks: pay for an engineer's opinion before offering.

**The building**
- [ ] Entrance, stairwell, lift, lighting: maintained or neglected? Neglect means unpaid service charges.
- [ ] Lift works, and has a maintenance sticker.
- [ ] Water tank and pump; ask about cuts and pressure.
- [ ] Concierge or guard: ask how many flats are short lets, about disputes, about the syndic.
- [ ] Noticeboard: arrears notices, planned works.
- [ ] Parking: titled space or informal?

**The flat**
- [ ] Measure the main rooms; compare with the advertised surface.
- [ ] Orientation and light; summer afternoon heat (west-facing rooms).
- [ ] Air-conditioning in each bedroom, and it works.
- [ ] Noise with windows closed: traffic, cafés, building works, a nearby mosque's loudspeaker at prayer times.
- [ ] Mobile signal; fibre available in the building.
- [ ] Electricity and water meters: individual, readings noted, no arrears.
- [ ] Kitchen and bathroom ventilation.
- [ ] Furniture and appliances actually included: photograph each for the inventory.

**For a short let**
- [ ] Can a guest find and enter it easily at night? Key handover or lockbox possible?
- [ ] Walk to what guests come for: time it.
- [ ] Pool, gym and shared areas open to guests, or owners only?
- [ ] Building rules and neighbours' attitude to short lets.
- [ ] Come back after dark and on a weekend.

**Before leaving**
- [ ] Ask the seller why they are selling, how long it has been listed, and whether any offer has been made.
- [ ] Ask for the last syndic statement and a utility bill.
- [ ] Say nothing about price on the day.
