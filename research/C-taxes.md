Research date: 18 November 2026. Scope: Moroccan side plus the UK interaction. All figures in MAD unless stated.

### a.1 Unfurnished / long-term letting ("revenus fonciers") — the regime in force for 2026 income

**Headline: the "flat libératoire 10% up to 120,000 / 15% above" regime ended on 31 December 2022.** Since 1 January 2023 rental income is taxed at the progressive scale after a 40% flat abatement. The 10%/15% figures survive only as non-final withholding rates when the tenant is a company or a professional.

| # | Finding | Source | Year | Quote | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a.1.1 | Net taxable rental income = gross rents less a 40% flat abatement (no deduction of actual expenses, interest or depreciation). | CGI 2026 art. 64-II | 2026 | "est obtenu en appliquant un abattement de 40% sur le montant du revenu foncier brut" | high |
| a.1.2 | The net amount goes into global income and is taxed at the progressive scale: 0% to 40,000; 10% 40,001–60,000; 20% 60,001–80,000; 30% 80,001–100,000; 34% 100,001–180,000; 37% above. | CGI 2026 art. 73-I | 2026 | "la tranche du revenu allant jusqu'à 40 000 dirhams est exonérée" | high |
| a.1.3 | The change of regime (from final flat rates to scale + 40% abatement) was made by LF 2023, effective for rents received from 1 January 2023. | NC 733 (DGI), section II-6 | 2023 | "la LF 2023 a réinstauré l'imposition des revenus fonciers dans le cadre du revenu global des personnes physiques" | high |

**Conflict to note.** PwC (reviewed April 2026) says rental income is taxed on gross at 10%/15% (paraphrase; https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/morocco/individual/income-determination). The CGI 2026 text, NC 733 and NC 737 say otherwise. Trust the DGI texts: they are the law and the administration's own current commentary, and NC 737 contains a 2026 worked example.

### a.2 Furnished letting and short-term tourist letting (Airbnb/Booking)

**This is the genuinely uncertain area. The written DGI doctrine and the local-tax law treat furnished letting as a professional (commercial) activity; much of the market, and at least one expert quoted in the press, declares it as revenus fonciers with the 40% abatement.**

**What is the practical regime for an individual with one Airbnb apartment in 2025–2026? Three positions coexist:**

1. **"Revenus fonciers" in practice** — declare gross receipts, take the 40% abatement, pay the scale, file by 1 March. Simple, widely done, supported by the Hespress expert. Risk: contradicts the written DGI doctrine (a.2.2–a.2.3); on audit the DGI could reclassify as professional income, deny the 40% abatement, and raise taxe professionnelle and possibly VAT.
2. **Professional income, individual** — register, pay taxe professionnelle (5-year start-up exemption), keep accounts (simplified regime) or use CPU if eligible; VAT-exempt under 500,000 if a.2.15 holds. Consistent with doctrine; heavier compliance.
3. **Company (SARL AU) at IS** — pushed by accounting firms for multi-unit or para-hotel operations (see g).

No DGI ruling, FAQ or circular specific to platform short-lets by individuals was found. **This must be confirmed with a Moroccan tax adviser before modelling a precise figure.**

## g. Company (SARL / SCI) versus personal ownership

- **Corporate tax**: 20% standard rate for companies with net profit under 100M MAD (the transition to 20% completes for financial years opened from 1 January 2026). CGI 2026 art. 19-I-A and 247-XXXVII-A — "20%, au titre de l'exercice ouvert à compter du 1er janvier 2026". Confidence high.
- **Dividends** to an individual: withholding 11.25% on distributions from 1 January 2026, 10% from 1 January 2027. CGI 2026 art. 247-XXXVII-C — "10% pour les montants distribués à compter du 1er janvier 2027". Confidence high. Combined Moroccan burden on distributed profit ≈ 28%.
- **Minimum corporate tax**: 0.25% of turnover, not less than 3,000 a year, with a 36-month start-up exemption. CGI 2026 art. 144-I. Confidence high.
- **VAT**: the 500,000 small-supplier exemption is worded for individuals ("personnes physiques"), so a company letting furnished property is in VAT from the first dirham (10% or 20%). CGI 2026 art. 91-II-3°, 89-I-10°. Confidence medium/high.
- A property company that itself lets the flat is not tax-transparent and pays IS (NC 737: "elle devient, par conséquent, passible de l'IS"). Confidence high.
- **When advisers suggest a company** (paraphrase of Nexora, LNT/Roche & Mameri, wafir): several units; hotel-type services; wish to deduct real costs, depreciation and interest and recover VAT; co-investors; larger portfolios.
- **Is personal ownership the normal route for one apartment?** Yes, on the evidence found: the DGI's guides for MRE and the mainstream adviser material all describe individuals holding and letting directly, and personal ownership keeps the favourable exit rule (20% of an inflation-indexed gain, minimum 3% of price) and the 40% abatement for unfurnished lets. A company adds accounts, annual filings, VAT and a second layer of tax, and for a UK resident the Moroccan corporate tax is not creditable against UK tax on dividends (last point from memory, unverified). Confidence medium.

## h. Worked example

Non-resident individual, one Marrakech apartment bought for 1,500,000 MAD. Calculations use the 2026 scale (CGI art. 73-I): tax = 10% × N − 4,000 (N ≤ 60,000); 20% × N − 10,000 (≤ 80,000); 30% × N − 18,000 (≤ 100,000); 34% × N − 22,000 (≤ 180,000); 37% × N − 27,400 above. Check against the DGI example in NC 737: N = 96,000 → 10,800. ✔

**Common assumptions**: no other Moroccan-source income; no family deductions; urban zone (TSC 10.5%); private tenants/guests (no withholding); turnover below 500,000; no personal use in the year (personal use would add TH on the rental value).

### h.1 Unfurnished long-term let, gross rent 84,000 a year

| Item | Calculation | MAD |
|---|---|---|
| Net taxable rental income | 84,000 × 60% | 50,400 |
| Income tax (2026 scale) | 10% × 50,400 − 4,000 | **1,040** |
| TSC (assessed on rents) | 10.5% × 84,000 | **8,820** |
| TH | let, not owner-occupied | 0 |
| **Total Moroccan tax** | | **9,860 ≈ 11.7% of gross rent (0.66% of price)** |

- If the lease recharges TSC to the tenant: owner's cost falls to 1,040 (1.2%).
- If the tenant is a company: it withholds 10% (8,400), credited; 7,360 refunded after the annual return. The 20% final option (16,800) would be a bad choice here.
- If PLF 2027's scale is enacted: income tax 0 (net 50,400 is under the 60,000 zero band).
- Sensitivity of the income tax to rent level (2026 scale): 120,000 gross → 4,400 (3.7%); 180,000 → 14,720 (8.2%); 240,000 → 26,960 (11.2%).

### h.2 Short-term furnished let, gross receipts 180,000 a year

Because the classification is unsettled (a.2), three scenarios:

| Scenario | Income tax | TSC | Taxe prof. | VAT | Total | % of gross |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Declared as revenus fonciers** (common practice): net 108,000 → 34% × 108,000 − 22,000 | 14,720 | 8,820 to 18,900 (see note 1) | 0 | 0 | **23,540 – 33,620** | **13.1% – 18.7%** |
| **B. Professional, simplified regime, real costs** (costs 50–60% of receipts → net 72,000–90,000; VAT-exempt under 500,000; years 1–5) | 4,400 – 9,000 (floor 1,500 after year 3) | 8,820 | 0 (exempt 5 yrs) | 0 | **13,220 – 17,820** | **7.3% – 9.9%** |
| B from year 6 | same | 8,820 | 8,400 (10% × 84,000) | 0 | 21,620 – 26,220 | 12.0% – 14.6% |
| **C. Professional, reclassified with few accepted costs** (net 144,000) | 26,960 | 8,820 | 8,400 | 0 | **44,180** | **24.5%** |
| C plus VAT denied exemption (10% inside the price) | 26,960 (ignoring VAT effect on base) | 8,820 | 8,400 | ≈16,400 | ≈60,600 | ≈34% |
| CPU, if a non-resident is admitted (not verified): 10% × (180,000 × 25%) + 2,880 top-up | 7,380 | (understood to be absorbed; unverified) | — | 0 | ≈7,400 – 16,200 | 4.1% – 9.0% |

Notes:
1. TSC base in scenario A: by the letter of art. 35-b it is the total rents (180,000 → 18,900); if the commune/DGI assesses on the dwelling's rental value (assumed 84,000, the long-let rent) it is 8,820. Which is applied to short lets is NOT VERIFIED.
2. Rental value for taxe professionnelle assumed 84,000 (normal unfurnished rent). Assumption, not an assessment.
3. Taxe de séjour is collected from guests on top of the price: e.g. 180 nights × 2 adults × 10–25 = 3,600–9,000 a year at the "houses let to tourists" range, or 720–1,800 at the 2–5 range. A host who does not add it to the price bears it.
4. Under PLF 2027's scale, scenario A income tax would be 8,720 instead of 14,720.
5. Platform commission, management fees, cleaning, utilities, syndic are not deductible in scenario A (40% flat abatement replaces them); they are in B.

**Central estimate for a deal model: about 15% of gross short-let receipts for all Moroccan taxes, range 8%–25%, with a VAT tail risk.**

### h.3 Sale for 1,900,000 after 7 years

Assumptions: personal ownership; not eligible for the main-residence exemption (it was let); flat 15% acquisition costs; no documented works; no qualifying loan interest; selling costs 0–2.5%; revaluation coefficient for a 7-year hold unknown in advance — the 2026 table gives 1.173 for a 2019 purchase (a period with unusually high inflation), so 1.10–1.173 is used.

| Step | Coefficient 1.10 | Coefficient 1.173 |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price + 15% flat costs | 1,725,000 | 1,725,000 |
| Revalued cost | 1,897,500 | 2,023,425 |
| Net gain (sale 1,900,000, no selling costs) | 2,500 | negative |
| 20% of gain | 500 | 0 |
| Minimum tax 3% × 1,900,000 | 57,000 | 57,000 |
| **Tax due** | **57,000** | **57,000** |

- **Result: 57,000 MAD (3.0% of the sale price)** — the minimum tax applies. Even with no revaluation at all the 20% charge would be only 35,000, still below the minimum.
- The 20% rule only overtakes the 3% floor when the gain exceeds 15% of the sale price, i.e. when the sale price exceeds about 1.35 × purchase price with no revaluation, 1.49 × at coefficient 1.10, 1.59 × at 1.173. Here the ratio is 1.27.
- Cash-flow: without an "avis préalable" the seller must lodge 5% of the price (95,000) within 30 days — 57,000 tax plus 38,000 provisional, the latter refunded if the DGI does not reassess.
- UK side: UK CGT at 18%/24% on the sterling gain less the annual exempt amount, with credit for Moroccan tax on the same gain. With a 400,000 MAD nominal gain the UK tax will often exceed the Moroccan 57,000, in which case the total is roughly the UK figure; creditability of the 3% minimum tax where Morocco computes no gain is NOT VERIFIED.

## Could not verify / needs a Moroccan tax adviser (and a UK one)

1. **Classification of a single Airbnb-type flat held by an individual**: revenus fonciers with 40% abatement (market practice, one press expert) versus professional income (written DGI doctrine in NC 717; taxe professionnelle nomenclature). No DGI position specific to platforms found. Drives income tax, taxe professionnelle, VAT, accounting duties and possibly the exit regime.
2. **VAT exemption under 500,000** for an individual letting furnished: text supports it; a 2011 circular excluded furnished lessors under the old wording. And the rate if liable (10% accommodation vs 20%).
3. **CPU / auto-entrepreneur eligibility** of a non-resident (health-insurance membership condition; residence condition; whether furnished/tourist letting is an excluded activity under decree n° 2-15-263).
4. **TSC base for short lets** (actual receipts vs assessed rental value) and the **assessed rental value** of any particular flat; treatment of mixed personal use and letting in the same year (TH exposure).
5. **Law 80-14 framework at November 2026**: whether the orders on home-stay / alternative accommodation have been published, the authorisation/classification required for a private flat, and any Marrakech-specific restrictions.
6. **Taxe de séjour**: which statutory category a private apartment falls in and the figure set by the commune of Marrakech; whether Booking.com collects anything; confirmation that the tourism promotion tax does not apply to unclassified lets.
7. **Platform data-sharing** with the DGI/Office des changes (claims that Airbnb reports every stay since 2024), and whether Airbnb charges Moroccan VAT on its service fees since June 2026.
8. **PLF 2027**: final enacted text (new IR scale from 2027; widened right of communication). Check the Loi de Finances 2027 and the DGI circular once published (usually late December / January).
9. **Property gains**: whether interest on a foreign (UK) mortgage counts in the cost base; whether a non-Moroccan non-resident can ever claim the main-residence exemption; the coefficient that will apply in the year of sale; whether a flat used under the simplified/real professional regime becomes a business asset taxed at scale rates on exit.
10. **Law 14-25 text** (2025 local-tax reform) was not read; rates/bases above rely on the pre-14-25 consolidated text plus press reports of no change. Payment deadlines for TH/TSC under DGI management not verified.
11. **Succession**: law applicable to a non-Muslim foreign owner (1913 Dahir, national law, renvoi), Family Code inheritance rules for Moroccan/Muslim owners, land-registry fees for heirs, form of will — notary advice needed. Items in e.6–e.9 marked "from memory" are unverified.
12. **Registration formalities** for a professional host (ICE, commercial register) — not verified.
13. **UK**: creditability of the Moroccan 3% minimum tax; deductibility of Moroccan local taxes; finance-cost restriction on overseas dwellings; property-income rates after April 2027 and any Budget 2026 change; Making Tax Digital thresholds; inheritance tax exposure — confirm with a UK adviser.
