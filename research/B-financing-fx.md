# B — Financing (mortgages, participatory finance) and FX / repatriation: Morocco

Research date: 18 November 2026. Scope: UK-based buyer of a titled apartment in Morocco; non-resident foreigner, MRE (Moroccan living abroad) and Moroccan resident compared.

## How to read this file

- **Confidence**: high = read in the primary/official text myself; medium = one good secondary source or an older primary source; low = marketing/comparison site only.
- **Quotes**: verbatim French extracts (under 25 words) are given for official legal and regulatory texts (Office des Changes instruction and FAQ, Law 31-08, Bank Al-Maghrib circular/communiqué, ministerial order, land-registry tariff). For press articles and bank marketing pages I give the figure and a close paraphrase rather than a verbatim quote (copyright limits); the URL lets you check the wording.
- **IGOC 2026 article numbers are inferred.** The official PDF's text layer does not carry the "Article N" labels. I derived the numbers by counting article headings and checking them against the instruction's own cross-references (e.g. the documents article cites "l'article 202" for the loan conditions; the loan article cites "l'article 201" for the definition, "l'article 11" for inbound settlement, "l'article 177" for sale-proceeds documents, "l'article 244" for the special account). Treat numbers as high-confidence but confirm against the printed PDF before quoting them in anything formal. Printed page numbers are given as a cross-check.
- **Search budget ran out** before sections (g) UK lenders and claim 10 (off-plan) could be sourced. Those are marked NOT VERIFIED and anything said there is labelled "from memory, unverified".
- Primary PDFs I read (official IGOC 2026 and 2024, BAM reports, Law 31-08, BAM circular, bank brochures, land-registry tariff) were fetched into the session scratchpad for reading only; nothing was installed.

**Practical consequence of the sworn statement:** a non-resident foreigner who already owns a home in Morocco cannot get a second dirham home loan under the general delegation (would need a specific Office des Changes authorisation). MRE have no such limit.

The 30% page is stale. One MRE guide (lesmre.com) says 80% "should be confirmed with your bank"; the caution is fair because banks set their own, lower, limits.

### c. Compulsory extras and consumer protection

**Law 31-08 (consumer protection), Title VI, chapter II "Crédit immobilier", art. 112–134.** Text read in the French version (marked unofficial translation; the Arabic text is authoritative): https://mahkamaty.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-31.08-%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%84%D9%83-%D9%81%D8%B1%D9%86%D8%B3%D9%8A1.pdf. **Implementing orders (in force 1 April 2016)** — secondary sources, medium confidence: early-repayment indemnity set at **one month's interest, not exceeding 2% of outstanding capital**; study/file fees capped at **0.1% of the loan** (La Vie éco, 16 Nov 2015: https://www.lavieeco.com/?p=87940 ; Crédit du Maroc booklet 2018, p.24; Consonews 2 Aug 2017). Order numbers not found. Note: art. 124 of the law ties the regulated fee cap to study fees a lender may keep when the purchase falls through; Crédit du Maroc presents 0.1% as the general cap. BMCI advertises free file fees; comparison sites quote 0–0.5% (low reliability).

**Is an "accord de principe" binding?** The law does not mention it (zero occurrences in the text). The only instrument with statutory force is the offre préalable. No Moroccan source was found saying an accord de principe cannot be withdrawn; treat it as a conditional pre-approval. Its typical validity (the video's 90 days): **NOT VERIFIED**. Practical rule: do not treat financing as secured until the offre préalable is issued, and keep the loan condition in the compromis.

### e. Getting money in and out

All from IGOC 2026 (official PDF above) and the Office des Changes FAQ (https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/faq); confidence high unless stated.

**Bringing purchase funds in**

1. **Accounts available.** Banks may open foreign-currency accounts and convertible-dirham accounts for non-resident (and resident) foreign individuals and for MRE (chapter VI §2, p.157). They are credited by transfers from abroad; debited for any payment in Morocco or abroad. Foreign banknotes can be paid in only with the customs import declaration (dated within six months).
2. **How to pay.** Inbound settlement must be by transfer received from abroad, by debit of a foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account, or by international postal order (art. 11, p.22). Contracts may be in dirhams or foreign currency (art. 3).
3. **Proof to keep — still called "Formule 2".** Art. 12 (p.22) lists the supporting documents: « Formule 2 : Achat de devises à la clientèle »; Formule 4 (debit of a convertible-dirham account of a foreigner or MRE to finance an investment in Morocco); debit advice of a convertible-dirham or foreign-currency account; exchange slip plus customs declaration for banknotes. The bank must issue the document and give the client a copy.
4. **No filing with the Office des Changes**, but keep the proof: the FAQ says foreign investors and MRE have no declaration obligation yet must keep the settlement documents. Without them the convertibility guarantee cannot be proved at exit.
5. **If the purchase falls through**, money brought in and not used can be sent back within one year on proof of the inbound transfer and non-use (art. 14, inferred no., p.23).

**Taking money out**

| Flow | Non-resident foreigner | MRE | Article |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sale proceeds, bought in cash from abroad** | Freely transferable if the purchase was financed in foreign currency: « un régime de convertibilité qui garantit aux investisseurs concernés, l'entière liberté pour le transfert » | Same, same condition | art. 170–174, pp.122–123 |
| **Sale proceeds, bought with a dirham loan** | Net proceeds transferable up to: initial foreign-currency contribution + principal repaid in foreign currency or from a convertible-dirham account + any capital gain (« et de la plus-value éventuelle réalisée lors de la cession ») | Same | art. 203, p.138 |
| **Part not covered by the convertibility regime** | Paid into a "compte convertible à terme", transferable in four equal 25% tranches (first at once, then one a year); or spend it in Morocco | Cannot hold such an account; funds stay in an ordinary dirham account, not transferable | art. 174–175, 241, pp.123–124, 170 |
| **Capital gain** | Transferable as part of the proceeds (after Moroccan tax) | Same | art. 171, 203 |
| **Rental income** | Transferable « sans limitation dans le montant et dans le temps, et ce, quel que soit le mode de financement de leurs investissements » | Only if the investment was financed in foreign currency, with art. 12 proof | art. 160–162, pp.110–112 |
| **Documents for sale proceeds** | Proof of original settlement (art. 12), sale deed and purchase deed, proof of taxes paid, proof of foreign-currency contribution and loan repayments | Plus proof of residence abroad | art. 177, pp.124–125 |
| **Documents for rent** | Ownership certificate, lease with certain date covering the period and showing the rent, proof of tax paid for year n−1 (to the bank by end-April) | Plus foreign-currency financing proof | art. 162, p.111 |
| Net of tax | Transfers are executed net of Moroccan taxes | Same | art. 8, 161 |
| Sale between two foreigners | May be settled directly abroad; the buyer inherits the seller's convertibility status | — | art. 174 |

**Points that matter for a deal model**

- **Repay the dirham loan with foreign-currency inflows**, not local cash. That is both the rule (art. 202) and what makes the principal transferable at sale (art. 203).
- **Short-term rental income:** the document list asks for a dated lease covering the period. How banks treat platform (nightly-let) income for transfer: **NOT VERIFIED** — ask the bank before relying on repatriating it.
- Using transferable rent (credited to a convertible-dirham account) to service the loan appears consistent with the text but is **my interpretation**, not something the instruction states; confirm with the bank.
- For MRE the regime is stricter on income and on non-convertible proceeds than for foreigners.
- MRE can also buy back and take out up to 100,000 MAD a year of currency they repatriated and sold in the previous twelve months (p.39) — a small side channel, not relevant to property proceeds.
